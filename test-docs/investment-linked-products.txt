Investment-Linked Insurance Products: A Comprehensive Guide

Part 1: Product Overview

1.1 What are Investment-Linked Products (ILPs)?
Investment-Linked Products combine life insurance protection with investment opportunities. Policyholders allocate their premiums between insurance coverage and investment funds. The investment component is managed by professional fund managers and the policy value fluctuates based on fund performance.

Key Characteristics:
- Dual purpose: insurance protection + investment growth
- Premium allocation between insurance charges and investment units
- Fund selection from a range of underlying funds
- Policy value depends on unit price of selected funds
- Transparency in charges and fund performance
- Flexibility to switch between funds

1.2 Types of Investment-Linked Products
Regular Premium ILPs:
- Fixed periodic premium payments (monthly, quarterly, annually)
- Suitable for systematic long-term wealth accumulation
- Higher allocation rates in later policy years
- Loyalty bonuses may apply after specified holding periods

Single Premium ILPs:
- One-time lump sum investment
- Higher initial allocation rate
- Suitable for clients with surplus cash for investment
- Lower ongoing administrative charges

1.3 Fee Structure
Understanding the fee structure is essential for proper client advisory:
- Premium allocation charge: typically 0-5% of premium
- Fund management fee: 0.5-2.0% per annum of fund value
- Insurance charges: based on sum assured, age, and health
- Policy administration fee: fixed monthly/annual charge
- Surrender charges: applicable during early policy years (typically 3-5 years)
- Fund switching fee: usually free for first few switches per year

Part 2: Fund Options and Asset Allocation

2.1 Common Fund Categories
ILPs typically offer a range of funds across different asset classes:

Equity Funds:
- Global equity funds
- Regional equity funds (Asia Pacific, Europe, US)
- Sector-specific funds (technology, healthcare, real estate)
- Emerging market equity funds

Bond Funds:
- Government bond funds
- Corporate bond funds
- High-yield bond funds
- Global aggregate bond funds

Balanced/Mixed Funds:
- Conservative balanced (30% equity / 70% bonds)
- Moderate balanced (50% equity / 50% bonds)
- Aggressive balanced (70% equity / 30% bonds)

Money Market Funds:
- Short-term government securities
- High-quality commercial paper
- Capital preservation focus
- Low volatility but minimal returns

2.2 Risk-Return Profiles
Each fund category carries different risk levels:
- Money Market: Low risk, low return (1-3% annually)
- Bond Funds: Low to moderate risk (3-6% annually)
- Balanced Funds: Moderate risk (5-8% annually)
- Equity Funds: Higher risk (8-12% annually, with significant volatility)

2.3 Asset Allocation Strategies
Advisors should recommend allocation based on:
- Client's risk tolerance and investment horizon
- Age-based allocation (100 minus age rule for equity percentage)
- Goal-based allocation matching investment timeline to risk level
- Regular rebalancing to maintain target allocation
- Dollar-cost averaging through regular premium payments

Part 3: Regulatory Requirements

3.1 Product Suitability Assessment
Before recommending ILPs, advisors must:
- Conduct a thorough Financial Needs Analysis (FNA)
- Assess client's risk appetite using standardized questionnaires
- Ensure the product matches the client's investment objectives
- Document the recommendation rationale
- Disclose all fees, charges, and risks

3.2 Disclosure Requirements
Product disclosure must include:
- Product summary sheet with key features
- Fund fact sheets with historical performance
- Benefit illustration showing projected values at different return scenarios
- Risk warnings and disclaimers
- Cooling-off period notice (typically 14-21 days)

3.3 Ongoing Obligations
Post-sale obligations include:
- Annual policy statements showing fund performance
- Notification of material changes to fund offerings
- Regular review meetings to assess suitability
- Prompt processing of fund switching requests
- Transparent reporting of all charges deducted

Part 4: Claims and Maturity

4.1 Death Benefit
The death benefit under ILPs may be:
- Higher of sum assured or fund value
- Sum assured plus fund value
- A multiple of fund value (e.g., 105% of fund value)
- Subject to policy terms and applicable deductions

4.2 Surrender and Partial Withdrawal
Policyholders may:
- Surrender the policy for current fund value minus surrender charges
- Make partial withdrawals (subject to minimum fund value requirements)
- Reduce premium payments while maintaining coverage (premium holiday)
- Top up investments with additional lump sum contributions

4.3 Maturity Benefits
At policy maturity:
- Full fund value is payable to the policyholder
- No surrender charges apply
- Option to extend or convert to a new policy
- Tax implications may apply depending on jurisdiction

Part 5: Risk Factors

5.1 Investment Risk
- Fund values can go down as well as up
- Past performance is not indicative of future results
- Currency risk for funds denominated in foreign currencies
- Concentration risk if allocated to a single fund

5.2 Insurance Risk
- Insurance charges increase with age
- Insufficient fund value may cause policy lapse
- Changes in health may affect cost of insurance
- Policy may terminate if fund value falls below minimum

5.3 Market Risk
- Economic downturns affecting fund performance
- Interest rate changes impacting bond fund values
- Geopolitical events causing market volatility
- Inflation eroding real returns
